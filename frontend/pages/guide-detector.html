AI detectors make mistakes

AI detectors guess from the finished words alone. When Turnitin launched its detector, it said 1% of papers could be wrongly flagged. Vanderbilt University worked out that across the 75,000 papers its students submitted in a year, that could mean around 750 false flags, and turned the detector off.

A Stanford study found widely used detectors consistently misclassify writing by non-native English speakers as AI-generated.

What to do if you’re flagged

  1. Ask for the details. Which tool, what score, and which passages.
  2. Collect your evidence. Version history, drafts, notes and sources. See how to prove you didn’t use AI.
  3. Offer to explain the work. Walk through your argument and your sources in person.
  4. Read the policy. Check what your school or client says about AI and about detector results.
  5. Ask for a decision based on evidence. A detector score alone isn’t proof.

Yes. They estimate from the words alone, and universities and researchers have reported false flags on human writing.
