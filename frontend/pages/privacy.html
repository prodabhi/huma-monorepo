Privacy

What Huma keeps, and who sees it.

Last updated November 2, 2026

Data Used to Track You

None.

Data Linked to You

  • User Content · documents, proofs and writing replays
  • Contact Info · the name you choose
  • Identifiers · wallet address, account ID, passkey public keys

Used for App Functionality only.

Data Not Linked to You

  • Usage Data · which Huma pages you open, steps you take like Start Recording or Share, and replays of how you move through a page, with every word hidden

Used for Analytics only.

Data Not Collected

Your email, fingerprint or face, location, contacts, browsing and diagnostics.

What Huma Keeps

  • Mac app. Pages stay on your Mac until you share one.
  • Web. Signed in, your documents are kept in your account and in your browser: the text as you write it, and once finished, its proof and writing replay.
  • Proofs. Key count, writing time and check-ins.
  • Writing replays. Every change you made, with its time, including text you deleted.
  • Passkeys. Only each passkey’s ID and public key. Your fingerprint or face never leaves your device.
  • Name and face. Faces are drawn from a short code. No photo is uploaded.

Typing checks run on your computer.

Shared Links

  • On the web, every finished document gets a link. In the Mac app, only pages you share.
  • Anyone with the link sees the text, its proof, your wallet address, name and face.
  • A link includes your writing replay unless you turn off “Let Readers Watch You Write”. Turning it off removes it from huma.social, but anyone who watched may have kept a copy.
  • A passkey’s public key is in each proof it approved, so readers can tell those pages came from the same passkey.

From Other Sources

World ID signs you in, on the web and in the Mac app. Huma gets an anonymous proof that you’re one unique person and makes your account from it. It never learns who you are.

How Huma Uses It

  • To keep your documents and make their links.
  • To set daily limits.
  • To let your Mac and browser share without signing in each time.
  • To see where people get stuck, so we can fix it.

No ads. No selling or renting your data.

Service Providers

Cloudflare stores documents and shared pages. Google Cloud runs huma.social. Cloudflare Turnstile checks the browser is used by a person. World ID, linked once to your account before you finish your first document, confirms one real person writes under it. We keep only an anonymous proof of it with your account and the pages you share, never your identity. Readers see a short fingerprint of it, the same on every page you share, so your pages can be told apart as yours. PostHog counts visits and steps and keeps page replays.

Your Choices

  • Delete a document on the web to remove it from your account and browser.
  • Delete any page on your Mac at any time.
  • Email us a link to remove a shared page, or to delete your account’s data.

On Your Device

Huma keeps your documents, sign-in and writing keys in your browser’s storage. Writing keys can’t be exported. No ad or analytics cookies. To count steps, your browser keeps a random number that isn’t tied to your account. Steps never include your text, titles, links or IP address. Replays show clicks, scrolling and the screens you open, with every word shown as stars; shared pages are never replayed. Both go through huma.social, so PostHog never sees your IP address. With Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track on, nothing is sent. Our servers may keep IP addresses for a short time for security. The Mac app checks for updates once a day and sends only its version.

Children

Huma isn’t meant for children under 13.

Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes too.

Questions

Email team@huma.social. See also our Terms.
