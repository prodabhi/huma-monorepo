# AI Detectors Are Biased Against Non-Native English Speakers

**Checked** = read in a primary or reliable source on 2026-11-05. **Unverified** = open the source yourself before publishing.

Use the CTA boxes from writing-rules.md in this post.

**Angle:** a fairness story, the one journalists and universities share.

### Facts
- The Stanford/Liang study (above): 61% average, and 97.8% flagged by at least one detector.
- **Rewording test:** when the researchers had ChatGPT enrich the TOEFL essays' vocabulary, false positives dropped sharply. People were pushed to use AI to avoid being accused of using AI. Check the exact figure in the paper.
- Vanderbilt cited this bias when turning detection off.
- Common Sense Media: Black teens reported wrongful flags at about twice the rate of others.

### Outline
1. Who writes "simply": second-language writers, students with dyslexia, technical writers, kids.
2. The study, explained plainly.
3. The irony: to look human to a detector, write like an AI.
4. A fairer approach: judge the process, not the vocabulary.
