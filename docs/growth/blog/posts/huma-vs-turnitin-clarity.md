# Huma vs Turnitin Clarity

*Turnitin now agrees that watching the writing beats guessing from the words. The difference is who owns the record.*

The most interesting thing about Turnitin Clarity isn’t what it does. It’s what it admits. The company that sold schools the most widely used AI detector now also sells a tool that records how students write.[1] That’s a quiet acknowledgment that guessing from finished text wasn’t enough.

I agree with them. But Clarity puts the record in the wrong hands. It belongs to the writer.

**Short answer:** for writers, Huma is the better choice. Both record writing instead of guessing, but Clarity is a paid add-on the school buys, students must write inside Turnitin, and the school keeps the record. Huma is free, the writer owns the proof, and anyone can check it, for school or anywhere else.

## What Clarity is

Turnitin announced Clarity at SXSW EDU on March 4, 2025, and made it generally available on July 15, 2025.[1] It’s a paid add-on for schools that already use Turnitin Feedback Studio. Students draft in Turnitin’s writing space, and teachers can see pasted text, typing patterns, time spent and draft history. It also includes an AI assistant. UCLA, for example, is trying it in the Winter and Spring 2026 terms.[2]

## Where Clarity is better

- **It’s built into Turnitin,** so a school can switch it on for every class at once.
- **Students don’t have to do anything extra** if their teacher already uses it.
