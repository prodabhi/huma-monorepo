# Getting Backlinks

People link to things that are **new** (data, a test), **useful** (a free tool or template) or **about them** (a badge, a reply to their article). Ordered by expected links per hour of work.

## 1. Pitch the writing-proof test (this week)

`/blog/ai-agent-vs-writing-proof-apps` is news: an AI agent tried four writing-proof apps and only one stopped it.

- **Who to pitch:**
  - Plagiarism Today (Jonathan Bailey already wrote about Grammarly's Humanize text being labelled as typed by a human).
  - Tech & Learning (it ran its own Grammarly Authorship test).
  - EdSurge, EdWeek, Inside Higher Ed.
- **What to send:** a 3-line email with the video link, the results table and one sentence on why it matters ("a certificate ID existed straight away").
- **Ask the other apps to respond:** email ValidDraft, LyteWriter and Grammarly with the results and offer a re-test. Their replies, and any re-test, give you a follow-up story.
- **Next, test Grammarly Authorship properly** with Authorship switched on, and publish it as a second post. That's the comparison people search for.

## 2. Original data (the biggest one)

Run the volunteer study in `backlog.md`: proven-human essays run through GPTZero, ZeroGPT and the others. Every journalist and researcher writing about detector errors needs a fresh number, and nobody else has proven-human text to test with. Publish it as a page with a table they can cite, and repeat it every term.

## 3. Things people link to on their own

- **"Written in Huma ✓" badge:** an embeddable badge for blogs, Substack and portfolios. Every badge is a link.
- **Free appeal letter builder:** students and advisers link to it.
- **AI policy template for teachers:** from `/blog/ai-detector-alternatives-for-teachers`. Writing centres link to templates.

## 4. University resource pages

Many university libraries and teaching centres keep "AI detection" guides (search `site:edu "AI detection" libguides`, or "Center for Teaching" + "AI detectors").
- Email the page owner: one line on what Huma is, a link to the teachers post, and the fact it's free for students.
- Ask to be listed next to the tools they already mention.
- `.edu` links are the most valuable kind for this topic.

## 5. Listings (an afternoon's work)

- **AlternativeTo:** list Huma as an alternative to Grammarly Authorship, Turnitin and GPTZero.
- **Product Hunt and Show HN:** see `playbook.md`.
- **Directories:** SaaSHub, and education tool directories such as the EdSurge product index and Common Sense Privacy evaluations.

## 6. Answer journalists

Sign up to journalist-request services such as Qwoted, Featured and Source of Sources, and answer questions about AI detection, academic integrity and AI writing with a quote and a link.

## 7. Your own channels

- Every proof link a writer shares points to huma.social.
- Make sure the shared page links back to the home page and `/blog`.
- Podcasts and newsletters about edtech, writing or freelancing: offer the test video as a talking point.

## Never

- Buying links, link exchanges or link networks.
- Mass guest posts on low-quality sites.
- Comment spam, or posting from accounts that hide that they're Huma.

Google penalises these, and the press hooks above are worth more.
